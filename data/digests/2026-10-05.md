# This week in AI governance: 6 new papers (2 peer-reviewed, 4 preprints).

## TLDR
- Major global players in AI governance are driven by conflicting worldviews, which could complicate creating unified standards.
- A new blueprint suggests integrating contracts, institutions, and standards to better manage AI risks and ensure compliance.
- Governance models for Human-AI collaboration are emphasizing the need to establish clear accountability beyond just human oversight.

## Global standards
- **Competing worldviews in AI governance: Directed content and critical discourse analysis of five official documents** [peer-reviewed]  
  This paper critiques various AI governance documents, shedding light on the differing perspectives of key global stakeholders.

- **The Global Enforcement Triad: Operationalizing AI Governance via Contracts, Institutions, and Standards** [preprint]  
  The authors offer a strategic approach to implementing AI governance, focusing on innovative contracts and compliance frameworks to mitigate systemic risks.

## AI risk & liability
- **THE EL-RAKHAWI DOCTRINE ON THE LEGAL LIABILITY OF AUTONOMOUS AI AGENTS AND THE "PIERCING THE ALGORITHMIC VEIL" THEORY** [preprint]  
  This paper presents a framework aimed at improving legal accountability for autonomous AI, addressing gaps in current accountability structures.

- **Human-AI Collaboration Needs More Than a Human in the Loop: Checkpoint-Based Governance (CBG)** [preprint]  
  The authors propose a governance model emphasizing checkpoints to ensure effective accountability in Human-AI collaborations.

## Public sector AI
- **Όροι σύμβασης για ανθρώπινη εποπτεία που αποδεικνύεται: υπόδειγμα συμπληρωματικών όρων για την προμήθεια συστημάτων τεχνητής νοημοσύνης από φορείς του δημοσίου** [preprint]  
  This paper introduces a model contract aimed at ensuring that public sector AI deployments include robust human oversight measures.

- **Tricky trade-offs on a transparency spectrum: how the Financial Times approaches transparency about AI use in news** [peer-reviewed]  
  This paper explores the Financial Times' nuanced approach to transparency about its use of AI in journalism, illustrating the balance between policy and practice.
