This week in AI governance: 8 new papers (1 peer-reviewed, 4 preprints, 3 other).

## TLDR
- A new framework for proactively assessing AI liability risks could help firms prevent harm before it occurs.
- Recommendations from AI governance research in Iraq present opportunities for regulatory improvements in developing markets.
- A comparative analysis of Japan and Indonesia’s AI regulations highlights the importance of ethical oversight.

## AI risk & liability
**حوكمة الذكاء الاصطناعي وتنظيمه في العراق: دراسة قانونية وتنظيمية ومقترح مؤسسي** [preprint]  
This study offers actionable recommendations for enhancing AI governance within Iraq's regulatory structure.

**讓決定留下名字:外顯、隱藏與照見的配置___機器心理學系列的治理收束** [preprint]  
The paper suggests strategies for creating a balance between transparency and opacity in AI governance.

**rbaa-sim: Reproducible Colab notebook for the risk-based attention allocation model of AI oversight in SMEs** [preprint]  
This paper presents a reproducible framework to manage AI oversight risks specifically tailored for small and medium enterprises.

**GAILC AI Liability Exposure Map (ALEM): Identifying Potential AI Liability Exposure Before Harm Occurs—An Eight-Domain Framework for Preventive Examination** [preprint]  
This framework enables proactive assessment of AI liability risks to prevent harmful outcomes before they arise.

**The hazard of fluency: a framework for governing AI liability in organizational decision-making** [other]  
This paper outlines a framework to handle AI liability linked to uncritical trust in organizational choices.

## Global standards
**AI Governance: A Comparative Economic Analysis of Japanese and Indonesian AI Regulation** [peer-reviewed]
