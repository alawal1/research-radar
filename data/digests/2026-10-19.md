This week in AI governance: 5 new papers (0 peer-reviewed, 5 preprints).

### TLDR
- A comprehensive registry of AI-related laws can help companies navigate diverse regulatory landscapes globally.
- New architectures for AI governance focus on ethical oversight, cognitive adaptability, and accountability.
- Enhanced regulatory measures for privacy and data protection are essential in addressing AI-related challenges in the EU.

### GROUPED BY THEME

#### AI risk & liability
- **Conceptual Neural Networks 2.0: A Distributed Cognitive Architecture for Conceptual Reasoning, Controlled Neuroplasticity, Collaborative Intelligence, and Ethical Governance** [preprint]  
  This paper presents a framework designed to improve ethical governance in AI through cognitive adaptability and separation of operational authorities.

- **GOVERNANCE OF A WAGER: On Stanford's AI Governance Lab, the Four Questions It Will Ask, and the Architecture That Answers Them** [preprint]  
  It outlines the necessary architecture to effectively tackle accountability and liability issues in AI governance.

#### Global standards
- **AI Law Atlas** [preprint]  
  This research introduces a detailed registry of AI laws from various jurisdictions, aiding in understanding and navigating global regulatory environments.

- **GAME OVER: Governance Architecture for Multimodal Agent Ethics, Oversight, Verification, Explainability and Regulation A Universal Governance Framework for AI Agents, Multi-Agent Systems and Autonomous Infrastructures** [preprint]  
  The paper proposes a universal framework for the governance of AI agents, ensuring ethical oversight and accountability.

#### EU regulation
- **Privacy And Data Protection Challenges in The Era of Artificial Intelligence** [preprint]  
  This paper addresses pivotal privacy issues arising from AI and recommends enhanced regulatory solutions and technical measures for effective governance.
