# This week in AI governance: 4 new papers (2 peer-reviewed, 1 preprint, 1 other).

## TLDR
- A new framework for AI governance emphasizes policy visibility and user recourse mechanisms.
- Legal reforms are needed in the EU to address gaps in existing AI liability doctrines.
- The integration of AI in Vietnam's public administration raises concerns about accountability.

## AI risk & liability
**Between Niti and Nyaya: A Society-Centered Framework for AI Governance** [preprint]  
This paper offers a novel framework focused on visibility in AI policy and the importance of user recourse.

**Emerging Technologies to Ensure AI Transparency** [other]  
The chapter explores innovative technologies aimed at improving AI transparency, which are critical for fostering trust and accountability in AI systems.

## EU regulation
**AI responsibility and the illusion of control: doctrines, gaps and the EU response** [peer-reviewed]  
This critique highlights the insufficiencies in current EU liability frameworks and calls for more robust legal reforms for effective AI governance.

## Public sector AI
**Artificial Intelligence and the Socialist Rule-of-Law State in Vietnam: Rethinking the Exercise and Control of Public Power** [peer-reviewed]  
This article examines the challenges posed by AI in Vietnam's public administration and stresses the need for strengthened control and accountability mechanisms.
